# Investigation of Space-Frequency and Spatial Domain based 2.5D Computed Tomography Texture Analysis using 3D-Printed Phantoms

<img width="4093" height="2303" alt="flowchart" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/76013d2d-2ad6-4aa2-9aed-912adf771386" />

Texture analysis (TA) enables the quantitative characterization of image patterns through the extraction of spatial and frequency-based features, with critical applications in medical imaging for diagnosis and treatment planning. This study investigates the reliability and robustness of texture features by employing both spatial and space-frequency domain analyses on 3D-printed phantoms. It introduces a novel multi-domain approach to TA, particularly through the incorporation of the Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Transform (DTCWT), which represents a significant advancement in radiomic feature extraction and standardization in the space-frequency domain, addressing the limitations of traditional spatial and frequency-based methods. DTCWT enhances traditional wavelet transform (i.e. Discrete Wavelet Transform) by using complex-valued, directionally sensitive 6-oriented Gabor filters (±15°, ±45° and ±75°) to decompose images into multi-scale, multi-orientation sub-bands with improved shift invariance and phase preservation, making it highly effective for texture and edge analysis.

To comprehensively assess image textures via 3D-printed phantoms with periodic patterns, TA is carried out using two approaches: 1) Space-frequency features are derived via the DTCWT. By single-level decomposition of CT images (slices) into multiple sub-bands using complex-valued Gabor filters, the DTCWT captures both approximation (coarse) and 6 detailed texture maps. 2) Spatial features are extracted through Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM), capturing spatial pixel relationships. 
Both feature sets corresponding to the respective domains are evaluated for the repeatability and reproducibility of CT scans using normalized Haralick texture features, including energy, contrast, correlation, homogeneity, entropy, variance, sum average, sum variance, sum entropy, difference variance, difference entropy, information measures of correlation (IMC1 and IMC2), and maximal correlation. These assessments are handled as part of the quality control process to determine whether both image acquisition by the CT device(s) and texture features extracted from the images (slices) are consistent and stable. In addition, reproducible code control has been ensured through unit testing to verify the consistency of features under identical input conditions.

The results are visualized through violin plots. [[Here some results]](https://github.com/ogokk/CT_phantom_texture_analysis/tree/main/CCR_4_honeycomb) The first scan for both coarse DTCWT and GLCM is used as a reference to monitor how much the median values deviate from the initial scan across all next scans, in order to make follow-up assessment and evaluate the performance of a CT device. [[Here some results-2]](https://github.com/ogokk/CT_phantom_texture_analysis/tree/main/Multi-violins%20(24%20CT%20Scans)) The comparison of feature distributions showed that, while the violin plots in the spatial domain is more stable and has a narrower range, the features derived from the space-frequency domain demonstrates a wider range of values. This greater variability indicates that the space-frequency domain offers a more sensitive and in-depth approach for analyzing texture features.

This multi-domain approach not only provides slice-level sensitive measurements and a comprehensive framework for analyzing texture features but also enables the assessment of the robustness and consistency of radiomic features across varying imaging conditions, including intra- and inter-CT scan variations.
