# Investigation of Space-Frequency and Spatial Domain based 2.5D Computed Tomography Texture Analysis using 3D-Printed Phantoms
Texture analysis (TA) enables the quantitative characterization of image patterns through the extraction of spatial and frequency-based features, with critical applications in medical imaging for diagnosis and treatment planning. This study investigates the reliability and robustness of texture features by employing both spatial and space-frequency domain analyses on 3D-printed phantoms. It introduces a novel multi-domain approach to TA, particularly through the incorporation of the Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Transform (DTCWT), which represents a significant advancement in radiomic feature extraction and standardization in the space-frequency domain, addressing the limitations of traditional spatial and frequency-based methods. DTCWT enhances traditional wavelet transform (i.e. Discrete Wavelet Transform) by using complex-valued, directionally sensitive 6-oriented Gabor filters to decompose images into multi-scale, multi-orientation sub-bands with improved shift invariance and phase preservation, making it highly effective for texture and edge analysis.
At this point, to comprehensively assess image textures, TA is carried out using two approaches: 1) Space-frequency features are derived via the DTCWT. By single-level decomposing CT images (slices) into multiple sub-bands using complex-valued Gabor filters, the DTCWT captures both approximation (coarse) and detailed texture information. 2) Spatial features are extracted through gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM), capturing spatial pixel relationships. 
Both feature sets corresponding to the respective domains are evaluated for repeatability and reproducibility using normalized Haralick texture feature values and visualized through violin plots. This multi-domain approach addresses not only current limitations in the field by integrating DTCWT, but also enables the assessment of the robustness and consistency of radiomic features across varying imaging conditions, including intra- and inter-CT scan variations.
